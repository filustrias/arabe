Verbs of Movement 1: Go, Come, Return

This lesson builds the three everyday verbs — go (راح), come (جه) and return (رجع) — plus the basic words for near and far and the prepositions you need to say where you’re heading. Tap any sentence to reveal its franco and English.

How movement works

Three tense markers. Egyptian Arabic glues a prefix onto the verb to mark tense. The present takes بـ (be-) for a habit or an action happening now (baru7 = “I go / I’m going”). The future takes هـ (ha-) (haru7 = “I’ll go”). The past has no prefix — it uses endings instead (ro7t = “I went”).

The three verbs are three different shapes, and the shape tells you what to expect. راح (go) is a hollow verb — its middle root letter (و) shifts around, so ro7t (“I went”) barely resembles ra7 (“he went”). جه (come) is irregular — its present ييجي looks unrelated to its past جه, so just memorise both. رجع (return) is a regular sound verb — the stem stays put (rege3 / yirga3), making it the easiest of the three.

The participle — your most useful shortcut. Instead of the full present, Egyptians very often use an adjective-like form to say “(I’m) on my way”: رايح (raye7) going, جاي (gayy) coming, راجع (rage3) heading back. It agrees in gender and number, and it’s how you actually say “I’m coming!” — ana gayy!

Saying where. Use لـ (le) = “to”, or just drop in the bare place (raye7 esh-sho8l = “going to work”). من (men) = “from”.

Examples

The same verb across the three tenses.

أنا بروح الشغل كل يوم ana baru7 esh-sho8l kol yom I go to work every day (habit — بـ)
أنا هروح الشغل بكرة ana haru7 esh-sho8l bukra I’ll go to work tomorrow (future — هـ)
أنا رحت الشغل إمبارح ana ro7t esh-sho8l embare7 I went to work yesterday (past — endings)

The participle: “on my way”

This replaces the full present in everyday speech. It agrees with the speaker in gender and number.

Verb Participle (m. / f. / pl.) - franco Meaning
راح رايح / رايحة / رايحين - raye7 / ray7a / ray7een going / on the way to
جه جاي / جاية / جايين - gayy / gayya / gayyeen coming / on the way
رجع راجع / راجعة / راجعين - rage3 / rag3a / rag3een returning / on the way back
أنا جاي! ana gayy! I’m coming! (right now)
إحنا راجعين البيت e7na rag3een el-bet we’re on our way back home

Place words: here, there, near, far

Arabic - franco English
هنا - hena here
هناك - henak there / over there
قريب - 2orayyeb near / close
بعيد - be3eed far

For “way over there”, say هناك بعيد (henak be3eed) — literally “there, far”.

Dialogue: “Where are you going?”

Karim catches Mona on her way out.

كريم — Karim
رايحة فين يا منى؟ ray7a feen ya Mona? Where are you going, Mona?
منى — Mona
رايحة الشغل. إنت جاي معايا؟ ray7a esh-sho8l. inta gayy ma3aya? I’m going to work. Are you coming with me?
كريم — Karim
لأ، أنا هروح بعدين. الشغل بعيد؟ la2, ana haru7 ba3dein. esh-sho8l be3eed? No, I’ll go later. Is work far?
منى — Mona
لأ، قريب من البيت. la2, 2orayyeb men el-bet. No, it’s near the house.
كريم — Karim
هترجعي امتى؟ hatirga3i emta? When will you come back?
منى — Mona
هرجع بالليل. harga3 bel-leil. I’ll come back at night.
