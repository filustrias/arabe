Negation

Egyptian has two ways to say “not”, and choosing between them comes down to one question: is the thing you’re negating a verb or not? Tap any example to reveal its franco and English.

The rule in one line

  • Not a verb (a noun, adjective, participle, place) → put مش (mesh) in front.
  • A verb (present or past) → wrap ما…ش (ma…sh) around it.

مش — for everything that isn’t a verb

Nominal sentences, adjectives, participles, prepositional phrases — all take a single مش in front of the negated part.

أنا مش دكتور ana mesh doktor I’m not a doctor (nominal)
الأكل مش حلو el-akl mesh 7elw the food isn’t nice (adjective)
مش عايز قهوة mesh 3ayez 2ahwa I don’t want coffee (participle)
أنا مش هنا ana mesh hena I’m not here (place)

Remember the participle catch from the present-tense chapter: عايز، عارف، فاهم look like “I want / know / understand”, but they’re not verbs, so they take مش — never ما…ش.

ما…ش — wrapping a verb

A real verb gets ما (ma) clipped to the front and ش (sh) to the back. In the present, the whole بـ form sits inside the wrap. Here it is on كتب (katab) “to write”:

Pronoun Affirmative Negative
أنا - ana بكتب - baktib مبكتبش - mabaktibsh
إنت - inta بتكتب - bitiktib مبتكتبش - mabtiktibsh
إنتِ - inti بتكتبي - bitiktibi مبتكتبيش - mabtiktibeesh
هو - huwwa بيكتب - biyiktib مبيكتبش - mabyiktibsh
هي - hiyya بتكتب - bitiktib مبتكتبش - mabtiktibsh
إحنا - e7na بنكتب - biniktib مبنكتبش - mabniktibsh
إنتو - intu بتكتبوا - bitiktibu مبتكتبوش - mabtiktiboosh
هم - humma بيكتبوا - biyiktibu مبيكتبوش - mabyiktiboosh

The بـ never leaves — baktib just gets bracketed: ma‑baktib‑sh.

ما…ش in the past

Same wrap, but the past has no بـ inside, so ما…ش goes straight onto the bare verb:

ماكتبش الرسالة makatabsh el-resala he didn’t write the letter
ماراحش الشغل امبارح maraa7sh el-sho8l embare7 he didn’t go to work yesterday
منمتش بدري manamtesh badri I didn’t sleep early

Two you’ll hear constantly

A small set of very common words drop the بـ and negate the bare stem. The most important is عرف (3eref) “to know” — the everyday “I don’t know” is معرفش (ma3rafsh), straight from Samar’s notes:

Pronoun Negative English
أنا - ana معرفش - ma3rafsh I don’t know
إنت - inta متعرفش - mati3rafsh you (m.) don’t know
إنتِ - inti متعرفيش - mati3rafeesh you (f.) don’t know
هو - huwwa ميعرفش - mayi3rafsh he doesn’t know
هي - hiyya متعرفش - mati3rafsh she doesn’t know
إحنا - e7na منعرفش - mani3rafsh we don’t know
إنتو - intu متعرفوش - mati3rafoosh you (pl.) don’t know
هم - humma ميعرفوش - mayi3rafoosh they don’t know

The other is “to have” — عندي (3andi) “I have” → ماعنديش (ma3andeesh) “I don’t have”: ma3andeesh wa2t “I don’t have time”.

Examples

مبشربش قهوة mabashrabsh 2ahwa I don’t drink coffee
هو ميعرفش عربي huwwa mayi3rafsh 3araby he doesn’t know Arabic
ماعنديش وقت النهاردة ma3andeesh wa2t el-naharda I don’t have time today

Exercise 1 — مش or ما…ش?

Negate each sentence with the right tool.

1. أنا دكتور (I’m a doctor)

Show answer أنا مش دكتور ana mesh doktor — not a verb → مش

2. بشرب قهوة (I drink coffee)

Show answer مبشربش قهوة mabashrabsh 2ahwa — a verb → ما…ش

3. عايز آكل (I want to eat)

Show answer مش عايز آكل mesh 3ayez akol — participle → مش

Exercise 2 — Negate the present verb

1. بيشتغل (he works)

Show answer مبيشتغلش mabyishta8alsh

2. بنفهم (we understand)

Show answer مبنفهمش mabnifhamsh

3. بتكتب (you m. write)

Show answer مبتكتبش mabtiktibsh

Exercise 3 — Translate into Arabic

1. I don’t know.
