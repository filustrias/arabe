Family

The whole family (العيلة — el-3eila), plus how to say “my” with each of them and how to talk about who’s married. It leans on three things you already have: عند (3and) “to have”, the no‑“to be” sentence, and adjectives that agree. Tap any example to reveal its franco and English.

The core family

The middle column is the “my…” form — most just take the regular ‑i ending from the Pronouns chapter.

Arabic - franco my… English
أب - ab / بابا - baba أبويا - abuya father / dad
أم - omm / ماما - mama أمي - ommi mother / mum
ابن - ibn ابني - ibni son
بنت - bint بنتي - binti daughter
أخ - a5 أخويا - a5uya brother
أخت - o5t أختي - o5ti sister
جد - gedd / جدو - gedo جدي - geddi grandfather
جدة - gedda / تيتة - teta جدتي - geddeti grandmother
حفيد - 7afeed حفيدي - 7afeedi grandson
حفيدة - 7afeeda حفيدتي - 7afeedti granddaughter

Two of these are irregular with the endings: أب and أخ become أبويا (abuya) “my father” and أخويا (a5uya) “my brother” — and abuk / a5uk for “your…”. The rest behave normally.

The wider family

Arabic - franco my… English
جوز - goz جوزي - gozi husband
مرات - mirat مراتي - mirati wife
عم - 3amm عمي - 3ammi uncle (father’s brother)
خال - 5al خالي - 5ali uncle (mother’s brother)
عمة - 3amma عمتي - 3ammeti aunt (father’s sister)
خالة - 5ala خالتي - 5alti aunt (mother’s sister)
عيلة - 3eila عيلتي - 3elti family

And two useful collectives: أولاد (awlad) “children / kids” and إخوات (ekhwat) “siblings”.

Talking about who you have

To say you have family members, reach for عند (3and) plus an ending — no verb “to have” exists:

Arabic - franco English
عندي - 3andi I have
عندك - 3andak (m.) / 3andek (f.) you have
عنده - 3ando he has
عندها - 3andaha she has
عندنا - 3andina we have

So 3andi a5 we o5t is “I have a brother and a sister”, and 3andohom talat awlad is “they have three children” (remember the 3–10 plural rule).

Married or not

Arabic - franco English
متجوز - mitgawwiz married (m.)
متجوزة - mitgawwiza married (f.)
مش متجوز - mesh mitgawwiz not married / single

These are participles, so they negate with مش — mesh mitgawwiz, never ma…sh.

“Whose…?”

To ask whose someone is, drop مين (meen) “who” at the end:

هي مرات مين؟ hiyya mirat meen? whose wife is she?
ده ابن مين؟ da ibn meen? whose son is this?

Examples

عندي أخ وأخت 3andi a5 we o5t I have a brother and a sister
ابني زكي ibni zaki my son is smart
أختي عايشة في القاهرة o5ti 3aysha fi el-Qahira my sister lives in Cairo
بابا بيشتغل دكتور baba biyishta8al doktor dad works as a doctor
أنا متجوز ana mitgawwiz I’m married

Dialogue: “Do you have siblings?”

Wael and Amira compare families.

وائل — Wael
عندك إخوات؟ 3andek ekhwat? Do you have siblings?
أميرة — Amira
أيوة، عندي أخ وأختين aywa, 3andi a5 we o5tein Yes, I have a brother and two sisters
وائل — Wael
وإنتي متجوزة؟ we inti mitgawwiza? And are you married?
أميرة — Amira
لأ، لسة. وإنت؟ la2, lessa. we inta? No, not yet. And you?
وائل — Wael
أيوة، وعندي بنت صغيرة aywa, we 3andi bint so8ayyara Yes, and I have a little daughter
أميرة — Amira
حلو! اسمها إيه؟ 7elw! esmaha eh? Lovely! What’s her name?
وائل — Wael
اسمها ليلى esmaha Layla Her name is Layla
