The Definite Article and Agreement

A grammar chapter. It covers the definite article ال (el-) = “the”, one rule that surprises every learner, and a small trick that turns a phrase into a full sentence. Tap any example to reveal its franco and English.

The article: ال (el-)

“The” is ال, glued onto the front of the word: البيت (el-bet) = “the house”, القلم (el-2alam) = “the pen”. There’s no separate word for “a / an” — a noun with no ال is simply indefinite: bet = “a house”.

The rule that surprises everyone

An adjective comes after its noun and matches its definiteness. So if the noun is definite (has ال), the adjective takes ال too. Portuguese says “the” once; Arabic repeats it on every word of the phrase — literally “the dress the red”.

Arabic - franco English
Indefinite فستان أحمر - fustan a7mar a red dress
Definite الفستان الأحمر - el-fustan el-a7mar the red dress
فستان أحمر fustan a7mar a red dress (both indefinite)
الفستان الأحمر el-fustan el-a7mar the red dress (the article repeats)

With two adjectives, every one of them takes ال:

الفستان الأحمر الطويل el-fustan el-a7mar el-tawil the long red dress

The same move on a few more items — notice the article appearing twice in the definite column:

English Indefinite franco Definite franco
a black suit بدلة سودا badla soda البدلة السودا el-badla el-soda
a white shirt قميص أبيض 2amis abyad القميص الأبيض el-2amis el-abyad
a blue tie كرافتة زرقا karavatta zar2a الكرافتة الزرقا el-karavatta el-zar2a
a green skirt جيبة خضرا geeba 5adra الجيبة الخضرا el-geeba el-5adra

The trick: how a phrase becomes “is”

Arabic has no verb “to be” in the present. This makes one tiny difference do a lot of work: if the noun is definite but the adjective is bare (no ال), the adjective stops describing and becomes a predicate — it means “is”.

الفستان الأحمر el-fustan el-a7mar the red dress (a phrase — both have ال)
الفستان أحمر el-fustan a7mar the dress is red (a full sentence — only the noun has ال)

To negate that sentence, put مش (mesh) before the adjective:

الفستان مش أحمر el-fustan mesh a7mar the dress is not red
البدلة كبيرة el-badla kbeera the suit is big

The three cases at a glance

Structure Example Meaning
noun + adj (no ال) فستان أحمر — fustan a7mar a red dress
ال-noun + ال-adj الفستان الأحمر — el-fustan el-a7mar the red dress
ال-noun + bare adj الفستان أحمر — el-fustan a7mar the dress is red

قميص - 2amis shirt m.
بنطلون - bantalon trousers m.
جاكيت - gaket jacket m.
بدلة - badla suit f.
بلوزة - boloza blouse f.
جيبة - geeba skirt f.
كرافتة - karavatta tie f.
جزمة - gazma shoes f.

A note on pronunciation: “sun letters”

In writing, ال is always ال. But in speech, the ل blends into certain following consonants (the “sun letters”: t, d, s, sh, z, r, n, l…). So الشمس looks like el-shams but is said esh-shams, and السمك is said es-samak. Before other letters (“moon letters”) the l stays: البيت is el-bet. We keep writing el- throughout this book for simplicity — just know that your ear will often hear the next letter doubled instead.
