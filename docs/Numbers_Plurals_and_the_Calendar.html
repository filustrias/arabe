Numbers, Plurals and the Calendar

A grammar-plus-vocabulary chapter: how Egyptian counts, how nouns go plural, and the everyday calendar words — parts of the day, days of the week, the time adverbs, and the months. Tap any example to reveal its franco and English.

Numbers

The base numbers (you also met these telling the time in the Daily Routine chapter):

1–5 franco 6–10 franco
واحد - wa7ed 1 ستة - setta 6
اتنين - etnein 2 سبعة - sab3a 7
تلاتة - talata 3 تمانية - tamanya 8
أربعة - arba3a 4 تسعة - tes3a 9
خمسة - 5amsa 5 عشرة - 3ashara 10

The tens are عشرين (3eshrin) 20, تلاتين (talatin) 30, أربعين (arbe3in) 40, خمسين (5amsin) 50. Compounds run units + و + tens: wa7ed w 3eshrin (21), setta w 3eshrin (26), tes3a w arbe3in (49).

Counting things

Egyptian changes the noun depending on how many there are. Four cases:

Number Noun form Example
1 singular يوم - yom (a/one day)
2 dual, add ‑ein يومين - yomein (two days)
3–10 counting form + plural تلات أيام - talat ayyam (three days)
11+ number + singular عشرين يوم - 3eshrin yom (twenty days)

So the plural noun only appears with 3 to 10. With two, you don’t even say “two” — the ‑ein dual ending carries it: yom → yomein, sana → sanatein, kitab → kitabein.

Plurals are their own words

Beyond the dual, Egyptian plurals are mostly irregular (“broken”) — you learn each one with its singular, the way you’d learn a new word:

Singular Plural English
كتاب - kitab كتب - kotob book / books
يوم - yom أيام - ayyam day / days
سنة - sana سنين - seneen year / years
ساعة - sa3a ساعات - sa3at hour / hours
ولد - walad أولاد - awlad boy / boys
بنت - bint بنات - banat girl / girls
بيت - bet بيوت - boyot house / houses

Days of the week

The week starts on Sunday, and the first four days are literally built on the numbers one to four.

Arabic - franco English
يوم الحد - yom el-7add Sunday
يوم الاتنين - yom el-etnein Monday
يوم التلات - yom el-talat Tuesday
يوم الأربع - yom el-arba3 Wednesday
يوم الخميس - yom el-5amis Thursday
يوم الجمعة - yom el-gom3a Friday
يوم السبت - yom el-sabt Saturday

(يوم الجمعة, el-gom3a, “the gathering”, is the Friday prayer day and the heart of the weekend.)

Today, yesterday, tomorrow — and weeks

Arabic - franco English
النهاردة - el-naharda today
امبارح - imbare7 yesterday
بكرة - bokra tomorrow
بعد بكرة - ba3d bokra the day after tomorrow
أول امبارح - awwel imbare7 the day before yesterday
دلوقتي - delwa2ti now
الأسبوع - el-osbo3 the week
الأسبوع ده - el-osbo3 da this week
الأسبوع الجاي - el-osbo3 el-gay next week
الأسبوع اللي فات - el-osbo3 elli faat last week

The same الجاي / اللي فات (“the coming / the one that passed”) work for months and years: el-shahr el-gay (next month), el-sana elli faatet (last year).

Months

Egyptian uses the European month names:

Arabic - franco Month Arabic - franco Month
يناير - yanayer January يوليو - yolyo July
فبراير - febrayer February أغسطس - a8ostos August
مارس - mars March سبتمبر - sebtamber September
أبريل - abril April أكتوبر - oktober October
مايو - mayo May نوفمبر - novamber November
يونيو - yonyo June ديسمبر - desamber December

In practice people very often just use the month number: شهر واحد (shahr wa7ed, “month one”) = January, شهر اتناشر (shahr etnashar) = December.

Examples

النهاردة الخميس el-naharda el-5amis today is Thursday
بكرة الجمعة bokra el-gom3a tomorrow is Friday
عندي تلات أيام أجازة 3andi talat ayyam agaza I have three days off (3–10 → plural)
الأسبوع اللي فات رحت مصر el-osbo3 elli faat ro7t masr last week I went to Egypt
بصحى بدري الصبح bas7a badri el-sob7 I wake up early in the morning
